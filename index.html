URAP 2026: One Mind, Two Languages

Bilingual Sound Communities

Project Description

Austin is known for having a high concentration of Spanish speakers, yet Spanish-speaking density varies considerably across the city, with some neighborhoods being more "Spanish-saturated" than others. Since we know that the sound systems in our minds are continuously reshaped by exposure, we ask: does living in a more Spanish-saturated neighborhood leave a measurable imprint on how people speak and perceive speech?

Our goal is to investigate possible community-level effects on speech production and perception among Austin residents, focusing specifically on whether Austinites living in neighborhoods with a stronger Spanish presence tend to produce more Spanish-like pronunciation than those in lower-saturation neighborhoods. We will examine Spanish-English bilinguals alongside both English and Spanish monolinguals, which will let us see whether any community effects we find are specific to bilingual speakers or extend more broadly.

What You'll Do

  • Read, synthesize, and critically evaluate relevant literature
  • Design stimuli and tasks for data collection
  • Recruit and run participants in the Speech Learning Lab
  • Analyze data and produce visuals

What You'll Learn

  • How to approach scholarly articles and use a citation manager
  • How to translate a research idea into appropriate stimuli and tasks
  • How to recruit and run participants in a controlled lab setting
  • How to run Praat scripts; the basics of R Markdown

What You'll Need

  • A citation manager of your choice — we suggest starting here!
  • Gorilla Experiment Builder
  • R/RStudio
