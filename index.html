URAP 2026: One Mind, Two Languages

Bilingual Sound Communities

Project Description

Austin is known for having a high concentration of Spanish speakers, yet Spanish-speaking density varies considerably across the city, with some neighborhoods being more "Spanish-saturated" than others. Since we know that the sound systems in our minds are continuously reshaped by exposure, we ask: does living in a more Spanish-saturated neighborhood leave a measurable imprint on how people speak and perceive speech?

Our goal is to investigate possible community-level effects on speech production and perception among Austin residents, focusing specifically on whether Austinites living in neighborhoods with a stronger Spanish presence tend to produce more Spanish-like pronunciation than those in lower-saturation neighborhoods. We will examine Spanish-English bilinguals alongside both English and Spanish monolinguals, which will let us see whether any community effects we find are specific to bilingual speakers or extend more broadly.

What You'll Do

  • Read, synthesize, and critically evaluate relevant literature
  • Design stimuli and tasks for data collection
  • Recruit and run participants in the Speech Learning Lab
  • Analyze data and produce visuals

What You'll Learn

  • How to approach scholarly articles and use a citation manager
  • How to translate a research idea into appropriate stimuli and tasks
  • How to recruit and run participants in a controlled lab setting
  • How to run Praat scripts; the basics of R Markdown

What You'll Need

  • A citation manager of your choice — we suggest starting here!
  • Gorilla Experiment Builder
  • R/RStudio

Your Research Team

You will be divided into three research teams. Each team takes ownership of advancing a distinct component of the study, but ultimately, you are a unit working toward one common goal. You will work closely with your team members, but also should communicate regularly with the group.

🎙️

Production

Your team will develop two speaking tasks. The first measures whether participants experience cross-linguistic influence when producing sound contrasts in Spanish and/or English. You'll analyze this data acoustically in Praat and statistically in R. The second task elicits speech recordings for the Perception team's listening stimuli.

👂

Perception

Your team will develop a listening task where participants hear sounds and make judgments about them — measuring cross-linguistic influence in perception of Spanish and English sound contrasts. You'll build your stimuli from recordings the Production team elicits, then analyze and visualize results using R.

📋

Questionnaires

Your team is responsible for two questionnaires: one collecting background information on participants' language use, and one eliciting attitudes toward English, Spanish, and bilingual practices like code-switching. After data collection, you'll analyze and visualize the results using R.

Project 02

Perception-Production Training (NSF)

Project Description

Second language learning is challenging, especially for adults whose language systems are well-established. For pronunciation, this means that an individual's perceptual space — their mental inventory of linguistically meaningful sounds — is organized according to their exposure to and experience with their native or dominant language. When learning an additional language, they must reorganize the perceptual space to accommodate the rules and parameters of the new language.

The "NSF" project is a speech sound perception training project with three main branches. The first is concerned with individual differences in learners' cognitive skills and their ability to discriminate unfamiliar speech sounds. The second examines how learners' perceptual skills map onto their production skills — you may hear this referred to as the "Perception-Production Link." The third branch examines learners' development as they undergo six weeks of strategically designed perception and production training in an unfamiliar language.

The objective for the Fall 2026 semester is to complete data collection on the NSF! This means more than a dozen participants will come through our door several times a week for six weeks.

What You'll Do

  • Shadow a more senior researcher
  • Run your own participant over the course of the semester

What You'll Learn

  • How to collect speech data in a controlled lab setting
  • How to run participants in a controlled lab setting
  • How to use a data collection protocol
